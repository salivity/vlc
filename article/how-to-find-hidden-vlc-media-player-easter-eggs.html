How to Find Hidden VLC Media Player Easter Eggs

VLC Media Player contains several playful, hidden Easter eggs and novelty features intentionally tucked away by its open-source developers. These range from seasonal interface transformations and interactive animations to hidden retro playback modes and minigames. This guide explains what these Easter eggs are and provides straightforward steps to unlock each one on your computer.

The Festive Santa Hat Icon

Every year during the holiday season, the standard orange VLC traffic cone icon automatically wears a red and white Santa hat.

  • How it triggers automatically: The hat appears on the interface icon between December 18 and January 1, relying entirely on your system clock.
  • How to trigger it manually:
    1. Close VLC Media Player completely.
    2. Open your computer’s system settings and manually change the date to December 25.
    3. Relaunch VLC, and the Santa hat will appear atop the cone icon.

The Bouncing About Cone

VLC includes an interactive animation hidden directly inside its software credits screen.

  1. Open VLC Media Player.
  2. Click Help in the top menu bar and select About (or press Shift + F1).
  3. Locate the large traffic cone illustration on the left side of the window.
  4. Double-click or repeatedly click the cone icon. The icon will begin bouncing, spinning, or shifting horizontally across the dialog box.

The Video Puzzle Minigame

VLC features a built-in game filter that chops any active video playback into a playable sliding-tile puzzle.

  1. Start playing any video in VLC.
  2. Go to Tools > Effects and Filters (or press Ctrl + E).
  3. Click on the Video Effects tab, then select the Geometry sub-tab.
  4. Check the box labeled Puzzle game.
  5. Set the desired number of rows and columns to adjust the difficulty.
  6. Return to the playback window to click and drag the video pieces back into their correct positions while the video continues to run.

ASCII Art Playback Mode

A homage to early computing, this Easter egg turns your video stream into live, moving ASCII character art.

  1. Open VLC and go to Tools > Preferences (or press Ctrl + P).
  2. Click on the Video tab at the top.
  3. Find the Output dropdown menu (default is set to "Automatic").
  4. Change the selection to Color ASCII art video output.
  5. Click Save at the bottom, then close and restart VLC.
  6. Play any video file to view the entire stream rendered in colored text characters. To revert, return the Output setting to "Automatic."
