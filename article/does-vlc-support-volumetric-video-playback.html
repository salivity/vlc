Does VLC Support Volumetric Video Playback?

VLC Media Player does not currently support the experimental playback of volumetric capture videos. While VLC is renowned for its vast codec library and features compatibility with 360-degree and stereoscopic 3D content, true volumetric video relies on three-dimensional geometry, such as point clouds and dynamic polygonal meshes, rather than standard flat-raster video frames. Because of this fundamental difference in data architecture, VLC cannot interpret or render volumetric captures, requiring users to look to dedicated real-time 3D engines and specialized spatial players instead.

Volumetric Video vs. Standard Video

Volumetric video records a subject or environment in three dimensions, allowing a viewer to move around and view the scene from any angle with Six Degrees of Freedom (6DoF). This medium relies on formats that store geometric depth and texture simultaneously, such as point clouds, textured meshes, or standardized formats like MPEG-I Visual Volumetric Video-based Coding (V-PCC).

In contrast, traditional media players like VLC decode planar video streams encoded in formats like H.264, HEVC, or AV1. These formats store color values across a fixed two-dimensional pixel grid, which cannot natively represent depth coordinates or dynamic geometry without an external rendering pipeline.

VLC’s Current Immersive Capabilities

VLC has expanded its toolset over recent iterations, which often leads to confusion regarding volumetric compatibility. VLC natively supports:

  • 360-Degree Video: Spherical video mapped onto equirectangular projections, enabling Three Degrees of Freedom (3DoF) where the camera's orientation can be rotated from a fixed point.
  • Stereoscopic 3D: Side-by-side or top-and-bottom video streams designed for standard 3D displays or VR headsets, which create an illusion of depth but lack rotational or positional freedom.
  • Spatial Audio: Multi-channel and ambisonic audio tracks that adapt sound direction according to the viewer's viewing angle in 360-degree playback.

None of these formats involve the dynamic 3D asset generation or real-time spatial positioning that defines volumetric capture.

How to Play Volumetric Video

To view volumetric video files, playback must take place in an environment capable of real-time 3D rendering. Common alternatives include:

  • Game Engines: Unreal Engine and Unity, utilizing dedicated volumetric plugins or SDKs (such as those provided by Microsoft Mixed Reality Capture or Depthkit).
  • Specialized Players: Dedicated software such as Arcturus HoloSuite, 8i Player, or EF EVE.
  • WebXR Platforms: Browser-based viewers leveraging Three.js, Babylon.js, or WebGL to stream and decode volumetric assets natively in compatible web browsers.
