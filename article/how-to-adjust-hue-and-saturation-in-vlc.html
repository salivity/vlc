How to Adjust Hue and Saturation in VLC

Adjusting the color balance of your videos can dramatically improve your viewing experience, especially when dealing with washed-out or poorly graded footage. VLC Media Player includes built-in video effect controls that allow you to manually fine-tune color properties in real time. This guide walks you through the exact steps to locate and adjust the hue and saturation settings for video output in VLC, both for immediate playback and as a persistent default.

Adjusting Hue and Saturation in Real Time

To change the color balance of a video currently playing:

  1. Open VLC Media Player and start playing your video.
  2. Click on Tools in the top menu bar and select Effects and Filters (or press Ctrl + E on Windows/Linux, Cmd + E on macOS).
  3. In the window that appears, navigate to the Video Effects tab.
  4. Under the Essential sub-tab, locate and check the box labeled Image adjust.
  5. Locate the Hue and Saturation sliders:
    • Hue: Move this slider left or right to shift the overall color tint across the spectrum (useful for correcting unnatural skin tones or color casts).
    • Saturation: Move this slider to the left to decrease color intensity (shifting toward grayscale) or to the right to make colors deeper and more vibrant.
  6. The changes apply to your video immediately. Once you are satisfied with the look, click Close.

Making the Settings Permanent Across All Videos

By default, VLC resets these adjustments once the application is restarted. If you want VLC to always apply your custom hue and saturation values to every video:

  1. Click on Tools in the top menu and select Preferences (or press Ctrl + P).
  2. In the bottom-left corner under Show settings, select the All radio button to open advanced preferences.
  3. In the left sidebar, navigate to Video > Filters.
  4. In the main pane, find and check the box for Image properties filter (under the video filter module list).
  5. In the left sidebar, expand the Filters menu (click the arrow next to it) and select Image adjust.
  6. Enter your preferred default values for Hue (measured in degrees from 0 to 360) and Saturation (standard value is 1.00).
  7. Click Save to confirm your changes.
