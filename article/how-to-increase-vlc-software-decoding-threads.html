How to Increase VLC Software Decoding Threads

Increasing the thread count for software decoding in VLC Media Player allows the application to utilize more of your CPU's cores, significantly reducing lag, stutter, and frame drops during high-resolution video playback. By default, VLC attempts to manage thread allocation automatically, but manually defining a higher thread count can resolve performance bottlenecks on multi-core processors. This guide provides direct, step-by-step instructions to adjust this setting through VLC's advanced preferences.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Open Preferences: Launch VLC Media Player, click on the Tools menu at the top, and select Preferences (or press Ctrl + P on Windows/Linux).
  2. Show Advanced Settings: At the bottom-left corner of the Preferences window, locate the Show settings section and change the selection from Simple to All.
  3. Navigate to FFmpeg Settings: In the left sidebar, expand the Input / Codecs category. Under this category, expand Video codecs, and then click on FFmpeg.
  4. Modify the Thread Count: In the right-hand panel, scroll down to the Decoding section and locate the Threads setting.
  5. Enter Desired Threads: By default, the value is set to 0 (which enables auto-detection). Change this number to match or exceed the number of logical cores (threads) your CPU has—typically 4, 8, or 16 depending on your processor.
  6. Save and Restart: Click the Save button at the bottom right. Close VLC completely and relaunch it for the changes to take effect.

Setting the value to the specific number of physical or logical cores of your system ensures that VLC's FFmpeg decoder maximizes multi-threaded video processing, preventing video freezing on high-bitrate media files.
