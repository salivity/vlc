How to Fix VLC Video Playing but No Sound

When VLC Media Player plays a video smoothly but produces no audio, the issue typically stems from accidental muting, incorrect audio output device selection, outdated sound drivers, or corrupted player preferences. This guide provides direct, step-by-step solutions to diagnose and fix the VLC no-sound problem on your computer, ranging from quick interface adjustments to configuration resets.

1. Check Basic Audio and Mute Settings

  • Unmute in VLC: Look at the bottom-right corner of the VLC interface. Ensure the volume slider is up and the speaker icon does not have a red cross over it. Press M on your keyboard to toggle mute.
  • Check the System Volume Mixer: On Windows, right-click the speaker icon in the taskbar, select Open Volume Mixer, and confirm that the VLC slider is turned up and not muted independently of the master volume. On macOS, check the volume controls in the menu bar and system settings.

2. Select the Correct Audio Track

Corrupted or multi-language files often default to a disabled or empty audio stream.

  1. Play the video in VLC.
  2. Click Audio in the top menu bar.
  3. Hover over Audio Track and make sure Disable is not checked. If multiple tracks are available (e.g., Track 1, Track 2), switch between them to see if sound restores.

3. Change the Audio Output Module

Forcing VLC to use a specific audio device or output method resolves most compatibility conflicts.

  1. In VLC, go to Tools > Preferences (or press Ctrl + P / Cmd + ,).
  2. Click the Audio tab at the top.
  3. Ensure the Enable audio box is checked.
  4. Under the Output section, change the Output module dropdown from Default or Automatic to DirectX audio output or WaveOut audio output (on Windows).
  5. In the Device dropdown, select your active playback hardware (such as your specific speakers or headphones) instead of Default.
  6. Click Save, then close and restart VLC.

4. Reset VLC Preferences and Cache

Improper configurations can silently break audio output. Resetting settings restores default functionality.

  1. Go to Tools > Preferences.
  2. At the bottom of the window, click the Reset Preferences button.
  3. Confirm by clicking OK, then restart VLC and play the file again.

5. Disable Audio Enhancements (Windows)

System-level audio enhancements can cause conflicts with VLC’s internal decoders.

  1. Press Windows Key + R, type mmsys.cpl, and press Enter.
  2. Right-click your default playback device and select Properties.
  3. Go to the Enhancements tab (or Advanced depending on your driver).
  4. Check Disable all enhancements (or uncheck Enable audio enhancements).
  5. Click Apply, then OK, and restart your media file.

6. Update Audio Drivers and VLC

  • Update VLC: Click Help > Check for Updates within the player to ensure you are running the latest version with current codec libraries.
  • Update Drivers: Outdated sound card drivers frequently fail to communicate with VLC. Open Device Manager on Windows, expand Sound, video and game controllers, right-click your primary audio device, and choose Update driver.
