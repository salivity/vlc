Limit Outgoing Streaming Bandwidth in VLC

Limiting the outgoing bandwidth of a network stream in VLC Media Player ensures your stream does not saturate your network connection or exceed the capacity of remote viewers. Because VLC regulates network usage through its transcoding engine rather than an internal raw network throttle, you cap the output bandwidth by calculating and assigning specific video and audio bitrates during the stream setup. This guide explains how to configure these limits through both the VLC graphical interface and the command-line interface.

Method 1: Limiting Bandwidth via the GUI

  1. Open the Stream Menu

    • Launch VLC Media Player.
    • Click Media on the top menu bar and select Stream... (or press Ctrl + S).

  2. Select the Source Media

    • Under the File tab, click Add... to select your file, or switch to the Capture Device or Network tabs to choose a live source.
    • Click the Stream button at the bottom.

  3. Configure the Destination

    • Click Next on the initial setup screen.
    • Under New destination, choose your streaming protocol (e.g., HTTP, RTSP, or UDP), click Add, and enter the required port and path parameters.
    • Click Next.

  4. Adjust Bitrate Settings (Bandwidth Cap)

    • Ensure the Activate Transcoding checkbox is enabled.
    • Select an appropriate profile (e.g., Video - H.264 + MP3 (MP4)) from the dropdown, then click the wrench icon (Edit selected profile).
    • Go to the Video codec tab, check the Encoding parameters sub-tab, and set the Bitrate field (e.g., 1500 kb/s).
    • Go to the Audio codec tab and set the Bitrate field (e.g., 128 kb/s).
    • Click Save.

  5. Start the Stream

    • Click Next, review the generated stream output string, and click Stream.
    • The total maximum outgoing bandwidth will now stay close to the sum of the audio and video bitrates plus a minor container overhead (approximately 1.7 Mb/s in the example above).

Method 2: Limiting Bandwidth via the Command Line

If you are running VLC in headless environments or using automation scripts, you can enforce bandwidth ceilings directly with the --sout transcode parameters:

  • vb: Video bitrate in kilobits per second (kb/s).
  • ab: Audio bitrate in kilobits per second (kb/s).

Command Syntax:

vlc input.mp4 --sout '#transcode{vcodec=h264,vb=1200,acodec=mp3,ab=128}:standard{access=http,mux=ts,dst=:8080}' --no-sout-all

In this command, the maximum theoretical output bandwidth is capped near 1328 kb/s (1200 kb/s video + 128 kb/s audio).

Strict Packet-Level Throttling

Because VLC sets bandwidth by encoding target bitrates, sudden motion or complex frames can occasionally cause temporary bitrate spikes. If your network requires a strict, hard-ceiling bandwidth cap that cannot be exceeded under any circumstances, use an operating-system-level traffic shaper in conjunction with VLC:

  • Linux: Use the tc (Traffic Control) utility with a token bucket filter (tbf) applied to the outgoing interface or VLC's specific port.
  • Windows: Use the Local Group Policy Editor (gpedit.msc) under Quality of Service (QoS) Policies to assign a maximum outbound throttle rate directly to vlc.exe.
