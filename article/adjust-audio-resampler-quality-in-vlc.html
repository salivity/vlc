Adjust Audio Resampler Quality in VLC

VLC Media Player allows users to manually adjust audio resampler settings to balance playback fidelity and system performance. While VLC automatically handles resampling by default to match your system’s output hardware, advanced preferences provide access to specific resampling modules and granular quality controls. This guide outlines how to locate these settings, select different resampling engines, and optimize audio quality in VLC.

How to Access Audio Resampler Settings

VLC hides resampler options under its advanced preferences menu. Follow these steps to access them:

  1. Open VLC Media Player.
  2. Navigate to Tools on the top menu bar and select Preferences (or press Ctrl + P on Windows/Linux, Cmd + , on macOS).
  3. At the bottom-left corner of the Preferences window, locate the Show settings section and switch the selection from Simple to All.
  4. In the left-hand sidebar, expand the Audio tree.
  5. Expand the Audio resampler subcategory.

Configuring Resampler Modules and Quality

Once inside the resampler settings, you can customize how VLC processes audio streams that do not natively match your sound card's sample rate.

  • Selecting an Audio Resampler Module: Click directly on Audio resampler in the left pane. On the right, use the dropdown menu to select a specific engine. Common options include:

    • Speex resampler: The default and most stable resampler in VLC, offering a balanced trade-off between CPU overhead and acoustic performance.
    • Secret Rabbit Code (libsamplerate): Known for exceptionally high-quality bandlimited Sinc interpolation, ideal for critical listening, though it consumes more processing power.

  • Adjusting Speex Resampling Quality:

    1. Under the Audio resampler tree in the sidebar, click on Speex resampler.
    2. Locate the Resampling quality option.
    3. The value is set on an integer scale from 0 (lowest quality, lowest CPU load) to 10 (highest quality, maximum fidelity). VLC defaults to 4 for general playback. Setting this to 8 through 10 provides audibly cleaner high frequencies and reduces aliasing artifacts if your CPU can handle the workload.

  • Adjusting SRC (Secret Rabbit Code) Quality:

    1. If installed and selected, click SRC resampler in the sidebar.
    2. Choose your preferred interpolation type: Sinc Best Quality, Sinc Medium Quality, or Fastest Sinc Interpolator. For the best audio reproduction, choose Sinc Best Quality.

Applying Changes

After adjusting the parameters to your desired settings:

  1. Click the Save button in the bottom-right corner.
  2. Completely restart VLC Media Player for the changes to take effect on subsequent media playback.
