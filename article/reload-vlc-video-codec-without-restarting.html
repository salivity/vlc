Reload VLC Video Codec Without Restarting

This guide explains how to force VLC Media Player to reload its active video codec and decoder pipeline during playback without shutting down the application. Video artifacts, frame drops, and hardware acceleration glitches often occur during extended playback or after system wake-up. By using built-in stream-switching mechanisms, you can instantly refresh the video decoding module, clear corrupted memory buffers, and restore normal playback on the fly.

Method 1: The Video Track Toggle (Fastest and Most Reliable)

The most direct way to unload and reinitialize the video codec is to toggle the active video stream off and on. This completely tears down the active decoder instance and builds a fresh one.

  1. While the video is playing, click Video on the top menu bar.
  2. Hover over Video Track and select Disable. The screen will turn black while the audio continues to play.
  3. Immediately click Video > Video Track again and select your original track (usually Track 1).

VLC will immediately re-detect the stream format, re-allocate decoding buffers, and re-engage either your software or hardware-accelerated codec without losing playback position.

Method 2: Cycle Deinterlacing to Rebuild the Filter Pipeline

If a codec glitch is related to the rendering or post-processing pipeline rather than the base stream, forcing a filter rebuild can reset the video module.

  1. Click Video on the top menu bar.
  2. Select Deinterlace and change the setting to On (or Off if it was already active).
  3. Under Deinterlace mode, select any alternative option (such as Yadif or Blend).
  4. Set Deinterlace back to your preferred setting (or Automatic).

This process flushes the video output module (VOUT) and forces the decoder to re-negotiate frame handoffs to your display driver.

Method 3: Change Hardware Decoding on the Fly

If you need to switch between hardware-accelerated decoding and software decoding to resolve a crash or corruption:

  1. Open Tools > Preferences (or press Ctrl + P).
  2. Go to the Input / Codecs tab.
  3. Locate Hardware-accelerated decoding and switch it from Automatic to Disable (or vice versa).
  4. Click Save.
  5. Use Method 1 (Disable and re-enable the Video Track) to force VLC to load the newly selected decoding method immediately without closing the media file.
