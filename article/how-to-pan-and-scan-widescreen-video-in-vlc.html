How to Pan and Scan Widescreen Video in VLC

Watching widescreen (16:9 or 21:9) video on an older 4:3 display often results in horizontal black bars, known as letterboxing. VLC Media Player includes built-in cropping and zoom tools that allow you to apply a "pan and scan" effect, filling your 4:3 screen by cropping the sides of the widescreen image. This guide walks you through the fastest methods to adjust your video formatting using crop presets, keyboard shortcuts, and VLC's interactive zoom feature.

Method 1: Using the Crop Menu (Standard Pan and Scan)

The easiest way to format widescreen video for a 4:3 display is by using VLC's crop function, which automatically cuts off the outer edges of the widescreen frame to fit a full-screen 4:3 aspect ratio.

  1. Open your widescreen video in VLC.
  2. Click on Video in the top menu bar.
  3. Hover your cursor over Crop.
  4. Select 4:3 from the list of options.

The video will immediately zoom in to fill the vertical space of your display, cropping the left and right edges while preserving the original proportions of the content.

Method 2: Using the Quick Hotkey

If you prefer using keyboard shortcuts while playing video:

  1. Press the C key on your keyboard while the video is playing.
  2. Continue pressing C to cycle through the available crop ratios until the screen displays Crop: 4:3 in the top-right corner.
  3. To reset the view back to the original widescreen layout, press C until it displays Crop: Default.

Method 3: Using Interactive Zoom for Custom Panning

If critical details are cut off by the default center crop, you can manually choose which part of the video frame to view using VLC’s Interactive Zoom.

  1. Navigate to Tools in the top menu and select Effects and Filters (or press Ctrl + E on Windows, Cmd + E on macOS).
  2. Go to the Video Effects tab, then select the Geometry sub-tab.
  3. Check the box labeled Interactive Zoom.
  4. A small picture-in-picture window will appear in the top-left corner of the video.
  5. Click and drag the white rectangular selector inside the small preview window to pan across the frame and focus on specific areas of the widescreen image.
  6. Click Close on the Adjustments and Effects window when finished.
