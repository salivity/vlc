Can VLC Play Speex and Theora Streams?

VLC Media Player natively supports both Speex audio streams and Theora multimedia streams without requiring additional codecs or third-party plugins. This article covers how VLC handles playback for both formats, clarifies the technical distinction between Speex audio and Theora video, and outlines how to open these streams directly within the player.

VLC Support for Speex Audio

Speex is an open-source, patent-free audio codec tailored specifically for speech compression and Voice over IP (VoIP) applications. VLC includes built-in decoding and encoding libraries (libspeex) for this format. It can process Speex audio packaged inside standard Ogg containers (commonly using the .spx extension) as well as raw network streams transmitted over RTP or HTTP. Playback requires no configuration; opening the file or network address will automatically trigger VLC's internal decoder.

Clarifying Theora: Video vs. Audio

While frequently mentioned alongside Speex in open-media discussions, Theora is strictly a video compression format, not an audio codec. Developed by the Xiph.Org Foundation, Theora is typically paired with audio formats such as Speex, Vorbis, or FLAC inside an Ogg container (.ogg or .ogv).

VLC provides full, native support for Theora video via libtheora. If you encounter an audio-video stream utilizing Theora for video and Speex for audio, VLC will decode and synchronize both streams out of the box.

How to Open Speex and Theora Streams in VLC

To play a local file or an online stream encoded with Speex or Theora in VLC:

  1. For Local Files: Drag and drop the .spx, .ogg, or .ogv file directly into the VLC interface, or navigate to Media > Open File (Ctrl+O on Windows/Linux, Cmd+O on macOS).
  2. For Network Streams: Navigate to Media > Open Network Stream (Ctrl+N or Cmd+N), enter the direct URL of the audio or multimedia stream, and select Play.

VLC handles all demuxing and decoding internally, ensuring smooth playback across Windows, macOS, and Linux platforms.
