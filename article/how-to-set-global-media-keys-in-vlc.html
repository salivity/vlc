How to Set Global Media Keys in VLC

Configuring VLC Media Player to respond to multimedia keys globally allows you to control audio and video playback—such as play, pause, stop, and track skipping—even when the application is minimized or running in the background. By default, VLC only captures keypresses when its window is active, but you can easily map your keyboard’s dedicated media keys to system-wide global hotkeys directly within VLC's preferences.

Step 1: Open Preferences

Launch VLC Media Player. On Windows, click on Tools in the top menu bar and select Preferences, or simply press Ctrl + P. On macOS, click VLC in the menu bar and select Preferences, or press Cmd + ,.

Step 2: Navigate to the Hotkeys Menu

In the Preferences window, select the Hotkeys tab (represented by a keyboard icon) from the left sidebar or the top navigation bar. You will see a list of player actions alongside two key-binding columns: Hotkey and Global.

Step 3: Assign Global Keys

The standard "Hotkey" column only applies when VLC is the active window, whereas the "Global" column controls VLC from any open application across your operating system.

To bind your media keys globally:

  1. Scroll through the list to find the actions you want to configure, such as Play/Pause, Next, Previous, Stop, Volume up, and Volume down.
  2. Double-click the empty space in the Global column next to the corresponding action.
  3. A prompt will appear asking you to press the key combination you wish to assign. Press the dedicated multimedia key on your keyboard (for example, the physical Play/Pause key).
  4. Repeat this process for each media function you want to control in the background.

Step 4: Save and Restart VLC

Once all desired keys are assigned to the Global column, click the Save button at the bottom-right corner of the Preferences window. Close VLC Media Player completely and reopen it for the new global system hooks to take effect.

Troubleshooting Key Conflicts

If your multimedia keys do not respond globally after configuration, another running application (such as Spotify, iTunes, or web browsers like Google Chrome) may have exclusive control over your media keys. Closing conflicting background media players or disabling media key hardware flags in your browser settings will allow VLC to capture the keystrokes without interference.
