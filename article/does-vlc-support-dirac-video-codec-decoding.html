Does VLC Support Dirac Video Codec Decoding?

VLC Media Player natively supports the decoding of the experimental Dirac video codec. Developed by the BBC as an open-source, wavelet-based video compression format, Dirac was designed for applications ranging from standard internet streaming to high-definition broadcasting. VLC enables seamless playback of Dirac-encoded content without requiring users to install third-party plugins or external codec packs.

Built-In Dirac Support in VLC

VLC's capability to decode Dirac video relies on integrated libraries, primarily Schrödinger (libschroedinger), a high-performance C-language implementation of the Dirac specification. Earlier iterations also relied on libdirac, the original C++ reference implementation provided by the BBC.

Because these libraries are compiled directly into modern releases of VLC for Windows, macOS, and Linux, playback works out of the box.

Supported Containers and Formats

Dirac video data is rarely distributed as a raw bitstream. VLC supports decoding Dirac video when packaged inside standard multimedia containers:

  • Ogg (.ogg, .ogv): The primary and most common container format for Dirac video.
  • MP4 (.mp4): Standard ISO base media file format containing Dirac tracks.
  • Matroska (.mkv): Flexible container frequently used for open-source and experimental codecs.
  • MPEG-TS (.ts): Transport streams containing broadcast-oriented Dirac streams.

Dirac and SMPTE 2042 (VC-2)

Dirac's development led to the standardization of its simpler, low-delay profile as SMPTE 2042, commonly known as VC-2. VLC handles both the broader, experimental wavelet-based Dirac specifications and standard VC-2 professional video bitstreams.

How to Verify Dirac Playback in VLC

To confirm that VLC is actively decoding a Dirac video stream:

  1. Open the video file in VLC Media Player.
  2. Navigate to the top menu and select Tools > Codec Information (or press Ctrl+J on Windows/Linux, Cmd+I on macOS).
  3. Under the Stream Details tab, locate the video stream.
  4. The Codec field will display either Dirac or drac, along with the stream resolution and frame rate.

If a Dirac video fails to play, it is typically due to an incomplete download or an improperly multiplexed container file rather than a lack of codec support within VLC. Updating VLC to the latest stable release ensures compatibility with the most recent Schrödinger decoding improvements.
