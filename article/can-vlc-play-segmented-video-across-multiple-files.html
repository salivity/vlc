Can VLC Play Segmented Video Across Multiple Files?

VLC Media Player can play segmented video streams that are split across multiple files. Whether you are dealing with online streaming formats like HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and MPEG-DASH, or locally stored chunks such as split MP4, MKV, or TS files, VLC provides built-in mechanisms to read and play these segments sequentially as a unified stream.

Playing Segmented Online Streams (HLS and DASH)

For adaptive bitrate streams distributed over networks, video files are split into small chunks (often .ts or .m4s extensions) tracked by an index file.

VLC natively parses these master index files:

  • HLS (.m3u8): Go to Media > Open Network Stream, paste the URL of the .m3u8 playlist, and click Play. VLC downloads and plays each segment in real time without manual intervention.
  • MPEG-DASH (.mpd): VLC processes the XML manifest to pull and decode audio and video segments continuously.

Playing Segmented Files Stored Locally

If you have downloaded raw video segments onto your local drive without a streaming server, you can play them seamlessly using several methods:

  1. Using the Master Playlist File: If the folder contains the accompanying .m3u8 or .mpd file alongside the chunk files, simply open that manifest file with VLC. VLC will locate the local segment files and play them in order.
  2. The "Open Multiple Files" Feature:
    • Open VLC and navigate to Media > Open Multiple Files.
    • Click Add and select all your segment files. Ensure they are sorted numerically or alphabetically so they load in the correct sequence.
    • Click Play. VLC will transition from one segment to the next automatically.
  3. Playlist Drag-and-Drop: Open the Playlist view in VLC (Ctrl + L on Windows/Linux or Cmd + L on Mac). Select all the segmented files in your file explorer and drag them directly into the playlist pane. Double-click the first file to begin uninterrupted playback.
  4. Creating a Custom M3U File: If the segments are standard media formats (like .ts or .mp4), you can create a plain text file named playlist.m3u in the same folder. List each segment's filename on a new line in order, save the file, and open it with VLC.

Limitations to Consider

While VLC handles segmented playback well, performance depends on how the files were originally split. If segments do not end on closed keyframes (I-frames) or contain mismatched audio/video timestamps, you may experience brief audio pops, video stuttering, or slight delays during transitions between files. Additionally, if the chunks are protected by proprietary DRM (Digital Rights Management), VLC will be unable to decode the segments without the necessary decryption keys.
