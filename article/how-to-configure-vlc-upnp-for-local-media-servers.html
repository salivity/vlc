How to Configure VLC UPnP for Local Media Servers

VLC Media Player includes built-in Universal Plug and Play (UPnP) functionality that allows you to discover and stream content directly from DLNA and UPnP media servers across your local network. This guide details how to access the UPnP service discovery feature within VLC, configure advanced settings to ensure the plugin runs automatically, and troubleshoot common network connectivity issues to successfully view your media libraries.

Accessing UPnP Discovery in the Playlist

In most modern versions of VLC, the UPnP plugin is pre-installed and available directly from the user interface without requiring external extensions.

  1. Launch VLC Media Player.
  2. Open the playlist view by clicking View in the top menu bar and selecting Playlist, or press Ctrl + L (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Option + P (macOS).
  3. In the left-hand sidebar, scroll down to the Local Network section.
  4. Click on Universal Plug and Play.
  5. Wait a few moments. VLC will send an SSDP (Simple Service Discovery Protocol) broadcast across your subnet to locate active servers.
  6. Once detected, your local media server (such as a NAS, Plex, Jellyfin, or Windows Media Player sharing service) will appear in the main window. Click on the server name to expand folders and stream your content.

Forcing UPnP Service Discovery on Startup

If UPnP does not automatically load or you want VLC to always search for local servers upon launching:

  1. Click Tools in the top menu and select Preferences, or press Ctrl + P.
  2. At the bottom-left corner of the Preferences window, look for Show settings and select the All radio button to access advanced settings.
  3. In the left panel, navigate to Playlist > Services discovery.
  4. In the list of available services discovery modules on the right, locate and check the box for Universal Plug and Play (or upnp).
  5. Click Save to apply the configuration.
  6. Restart VLC Media Player for the settings to take effect.

Troubleshooting UPnP Server Discovery

If VLC fails to list your local media server, check the following common causes:

  • Windows Firewall Settings: UPnP relies on SSDP over UDP port 1900. Ensure that VLC Media Player has permission to communicate through your operating system's firewall on private networks.
  • Network Profile: Ensure your computer's network profile is set to Private or Home, rather than Public. Public profiles restrict local broadcast and multicast traffic.
  • Multicast/IGMP Settings on Router: Some modern Wi-Fi routers disable multicast traffic by default or isolate wireless clients from wired clients. Verify that IGMP Snooping or Multicast is enabled in your router's administration panel, and ensure client isolation (AP Isolation) is turned off.
  • Server-Side Permissions: Verify that DLNA/UPnP streaming is explicitly turned on inside your media server application and configured to broadcast to your local IP subnet.
