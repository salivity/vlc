How to Split Video Across Multiple Monitors in VLC

VLC Media Player includes a built-in "Wall" filter that allows you to divide a single video feed into multiple distinct segments across an extended multi-monitor desktop setup. By configuring the filter's rows and columns to match your physical display arrangement, VLC splits the playback into separate windows that can each be placed on a different screen to create a cohesive video wall.

Preparing Your Multi-Monitor Setup

Before configuring VLC, ensure your operating system recognizes all monitors as an extended desktop rather than mirrored displays.

  • On Windows, press Windows Key + P and select Extend.
  • Arrange your displays in the correct physical order within your operating system's display settings to ensure smooth mouse movement and alignment between screens.

Enabling the Wall Filter in VLC

  1. Open VLC Media Player and start playing your video.
  2. Go to the top menu and select Tools, then click Effects and Filters (or press Ctrl + E).
  3. In the Adjustments and Effects window, select the Video Effects tab.
  4. Click on the Geometry sub-tab.
  5. Check the box next to Wall.
  6. Set the number of Rows and Columns to match your monitor layout. For example, use 1 row and 2 columns for a standard dual-monitor setup, or 2 rows and 2 columns for a four-screen grid.
  7. Click Close to apply the settings.

Arranging the Video Windows

Once the Wall filter is active, VLC immediately divides the playing video into separate, individual windows corresponding to the grid dimensions you selected:

  1. Drag each split window to its corresponding physical monitor.
  2. Double-click each individual window (or press F11) to expand it to full screen on that specific display.
  3. If window borders remain visible, navigate to Tools > Preferences (Ctrl + P), select All under Show settings at the bottom left, navigate to Video, uncheck Window decorations, and click Save. Restart VLC for this setting to take effect.

Disabling the Wall Filter

To return VLC to standard single-window playback, return to Tools > Effects and Filters > Video Effects > Geometry, uncheck the Wall option, and close the preferences window.
