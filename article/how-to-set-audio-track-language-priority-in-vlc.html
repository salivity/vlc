How to Set Audio Track Language Priority in VLC

Configuring audio track language priority in VLC Media Player allows the application to automatically select your preferred spoken language whenever you open a multi-track media file. By setting specific ISO 639 language codes in the VLC preferences, you eliminate the need to manually switch audio tracks every time you play a video with multiple language options.

Step 1: Open the VLC Preferences

  1. Launch VLC Media Player.
  2. Navigate to the top menu bar and click on Tools (on Windows/Linux) or VLC media player (on macOS).
  3. Select Preferences from the dropdown menu, or press Ctrl + P (Cmd + , on macOS) to open the settings window.

Step 2: Access the Audio Settings

  1. At the top of the Preferences window, select the Audio tab (represented by a speaker icon).
  2. At the bottom-left corner of the window, ensure that the Show settings option is set to Simple.

Step 3: Enter Language Priority Codes

  1. Scroll down to the Tracks section near the bottom of the Audio settings page.
  2. Locate the input field labeled Preferred audio language.
  3. Type the language codes you want VLC to prioritize, separated by commas. VLC prioritizes languages from left to right.

VLC accepts standard two-letter (ISO 639-1) and three-letter (ISO 639-2) language codes. For example:

  • eng, jpn, spa or en, ja, es instructs VLC to play English first. If an English track is not found, it will look for Japanese, followed by Spanish.
  • jpn, eng or ja, en prioritizes Japanese audio over English audio.

Common codes include:

  • English: eng or en
  • Spanish: spa or es
  • French: fra, fre, or fr
  • German: deu, ger, or de
  • Japanese: jpn or ja

Step 4: Save and Apply Settings

  1. Click the Save button in the bottom-right corner of the Preferences window.
  2. Restart VLC Media Player to ensure the changes take effect.

When you open a media file containing multiple audio streams, VLC will now scan the file's metadata and automatically select the first matching audio track based on your prioritized list. If the file does not contain any of your specified languages, VLC will fall back to the file's default audio track.
