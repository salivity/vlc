Enable Water Ripple Effect in VLC Preferences

VLC Media Player includes a built-in visual effect that transforms your video playback into an interactive pool of water with moving ripples. While standard adjustments are found in the basic effects menu, enabling the persistent water filter requires accessing VLC's advanced configuration settings. This guide outlines the straightforward steps to locate, enable, and customize the water ripple video filter using VLC's advanced preferences.

Step 1: Open VLC Preferences

  1. Launch VLC Media Player.
  2. Click on the Tools menu at the top of the window.
  3. Select Preferences from the dropdown menu (or press Ctrl + P on Windows/Linux).

Step 2: Switch to Advanced Settings

  1. Look at the bottom-left corner of the Preferences window under the Show settings section.
  2. Click the radio button labeled All. This expands the simple interface into the full Advanced Preferences tree.

Step 3: Locate and Enable the Water Filter

  1. In the left-hand navigation pane, scroll down and click on Video.
  2. Click the arrow next to Filters to expand the category, or select Filters directly.
  3. In the main panel, scroll through the list of video filters until you find Water video filter.
  4. Check the box next to Water video filter to activate the module.

(Optional): If you expand the Filters menu in the sidebar and click directly on the sub-entry for Water, you can configure advanced parameters such as the wave intensity or propagation speed.

Step 4: Save and Restart VLC

  1. Click the Save button in the bottom-right corner of the window.
  2. Close VLC Media Player completely and restart the program. VLC requires a restart for changes made in advanced preferences to load the underlying video filter modules properly.

Interacting with the Effect

Play any video file. Moving your mouse cursor over the video window or clicking on the screen will trigger real-time liquid ripples across the frame.

To turn the effect off, return to Tools > Preferences, select All, uncheck the Water video filter under Video > Filters, and click Save.
