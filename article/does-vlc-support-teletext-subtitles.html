Does VLC Support Teletext Subtitles?

VLC Media Player fully supports reading and displaying subtitles embedded within a teletext signal, which are commonly found in digital television broadcasts (DVB) and transport stream (.ts) video recordings. This guide outlines how VLC processes these embedded signals, how to enable teletext subtitles during playback, and how to resolve common viewing issues.

How VLC Handles Teletext Data

Teletext subtitles are transmitted as digital packets alongside the video and audio streams, historically carried within the Vertical Blanking Interval (VBI) or encapsulated within MPEG-2 and MPEG-4 transport streams. VLC uses built-in teletext decoders (often powered by the zvbi library) to parse these data packets. Instead of requiring external subtitle files like .srt or .vtt, VLC renders the text directly from the broadcast stream in real time.

How to Enable Teletext Subtitles in VLC

To display subtitles transmitted via teletext in VLC, follow these steps:

  1. Open your video file (such as a .ts capture) or TV capture stream in VLC.
  2. Click on the Subtitle menu at the top of the window.
  3. Hover over Sub Track.
  4. Select the track labeled with teletext, which often indicates the language and the teletext page number (for example, "Teletext - [English] - Page 888").

Subtitles will immediately render on top of the active video playback.

Accessing the Full Teletext Service

In addition to dedicated subtitle tracks, VLC allows you to browse standard teletext magazine pages if the full service is present in the stream:

  1. Navigate to the top menu and select View.
  2. Look for teletext controls or press the teletext overlay toggle on the playback control bar.
  3. Enter the desired three-digit page number using your keyboard (Page 888 is standard for subtitles in most European countries, while Page 777 is commonly used in others).

Troubleshooting Teletext Subtitles

If the subtitles do not appear or cannot be selected, check the following:

  • Stripped Data During Conversion: If a recorded broadcast was re-encoded (for example, into an MP4 or MKV container) using basic software, the teletext data stream is often discarded to reduce file size. Only raw transport streams (.ts, .m2t) consistently retain full teletext PIDs.
  • Verify Stream Data: Press Ctrl + J (or Cmd + I on macOS) while the video is playing to open Codec Information. Browse through the listed streams to verify that a stream with the "Teletext" codec is detected.
  • Hardware Acceleration Conflicts: In rare cases, video output modules can conflict with text overlays. If the subtitles flicker or fail to draw, navigate to Tools > Preferences > Input / Codecs and temporarily disable Hardware-accelerated decoding.
