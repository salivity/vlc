How to Step Through Video Frame by Frame in VLC

Moving through a video frame by frame in VLC Media Player allows you to inspect fast-moving details, analyze specific scenes, or capture the exact frame you need. This guide explains how to sequentially step through video frames using native keyboard shortcuts, the graphical user interface, and how to configure custom controls.

Method 1: Use the Keyboard Shortcut (Fastest)

The quickest way to advance frame by frame in VLC is using the default keyboard shortcut:

  1. Open your video in VLC Media Player.
  2. Press the E key on your keyboard.

If the video is currently playing, pressing E will immediately pause the video and move forward by one frame. Each subsequent press of the E key will advance the video by exactly one frame.

Method 2: Enable Advanced Controls (On-Screen Button)

If you prefer using a mouse, you can display VLC's hidden frame-by-frame button on the main control bar:

  1. Click on the View menu at the top of the VLC window.
  2. Select Advanced Controls from the dropdown menu.
  3. A new row of four buttons will appear above the standard playback controls at the bottom-left corner of the window.
  4. Click the Frame by Frame button (the last icon on the right, displaying a small play icon next to a vertical bar) to advance one frame at a time.

How to Customize the Next Frame Hotkey

If you want to assign a different key for stepping through frames:

  1. Navigate to Tools > Preferences (or press Ctrl + P on Windows/Linux; Cmd + , on macOS).
  2. Click the Hotkeys tab at the top or bottom of the Preferences window.
  3. In the search field, type Next frame.
  4. Double-click the Next frame entry in the list.
  5. Press the key or combination you wish to use as your new shortcut.
  6. Click Save and restart VLC to apply the changes.

Stepping Backward: VLC's Limitation

VLC Media Player natively supports moving forward frame by frame, but it cannot step backward frame by frame. Most compressed digital video formats rely on keyframes (I-frames) spaced several seconds apart, making sequential reverse decoding resource-intensive.

To go back, press Shift + Left Arrow to jump backward by roughly 3 to 5 seconds, then use the E key to step forward to the exact frame you need.
