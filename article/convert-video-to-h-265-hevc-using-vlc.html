Convert Video to H.265 HEVC Using VLC

VLC Media Player can successfully transcode legacy video formats into the modern H.265 (HEVC) standard. This article explains how VLC utilizes its built-in encoding capabilities to compress older files—such as MPEG-2, AVI, and H.264—into the space-saving H.265 format, along with a straightforward guide on how to perform the conversion on your computer.

Yes, VLC Supports H.265 Transcoding

VLC Media Player includes native support for the x265 encoder. This allows users to re-encode legacy media into H.265 without needing third-party plugins or dedicated commercial software. H.265 offers roughly double the data compression ratio of standard H.264 at the same level of video quality, making it an ideal choice for reducing storage requirements.

Step-by-Step Guide to Transcode Video to H.265 in VLC

  1. Open the Conversion Menu
    Launch VLC Media Player. Click on Media in the top menu bar and select Convert / Save (or press Ctrl + R on Windows / Cmd + Shift + S on macOS).

  2. Add Your Legacy Video
    In the File tab, click the Add... button to select the older video file you want to convert. Once loaded, click the Convert / Save button at the bottom of the window.

  3. Select the H.265 Profile
    In the Convert dialog window, locate the Profile dropdown menu.

    • Look for a preset named Video - H.265 + MP3 (MP4).
    • If this preset is not visible, click the wrench icon (Edit selected profile) or the document icon (Create a new profile). Set the encapsulation to MP4/MOV, navigate to the Video codec tab, check the Video box, and select H.265 from the codec dropdown list. Set your desired bitrate or leave it default, then click Save.

  4. Choose a Destination File
    Click the Browse button under the Destination section. Choose the folder where you want to save the new file and provide a new filename, ensuring the file extension ends with .mp4.

  5. Start Transcoding
    Click Start. VLC will begin transcoding the video. The progress bar at the bottom of the VLC player will act as the rendering timeline. Do not close VLC until the progress bar reaches the end, as interrupting playback will corrupt the exported file.

Performance Considerations

Transcoding to H.265 requires significantly more CPU power than older codecs. Depending on your computer's hardware specifications and the length of the source video, the process may take some time. Enabling hardware acceleration in VLC's preferences (under Tools > Preferences > Input / Codecs) can help speed up the process if your system's graphics card supports HEVC encoding.
