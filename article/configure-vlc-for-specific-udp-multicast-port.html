Configure VLC for Specific UDP Multicast Port

This article provides a step-by-step guide to configuring VLC Media Player to listen on a designated network port for all incoming UDP multicast streams. By adjusting VLC's advanced input settings, you can override default port behaviors, ensuring consistent stream reception across your local network without needing to specify the port number in every stream URL.

Method 1: Set the Port via VLC Advanced Preferences

  1. Open VLC Media Player.
  2. Navigate to the top menu bar and click Tools, then select Preferences (or press Ctrl + P on Windows/Linux).
  3. At the bottom-left corner of the Preferences window, locate the Show settings section and switch the radio button from Simple to All.
  4. In the left-hand navigation tree, expand Input / Codecs.
  5. Expand the Access modules category.
  6. Scroll down and click on UDP.
  7. In the right-hand panel, find the field labeled Server port.
  8. Enter your desired UDP port number (for example, 5004 or 1234).
  9. Click Save to apply the configuration.
  10. Restart VLC Media Player to ensure the changes take effect.

Once configured, any standard UDP multicast address opened in VLC (such as udp://@239.255.1.1) will automatically bind to and receive data from your specified server port.

Method 2: Force the UDP Port via Command Line

If you launch VLC through scripts, shortcuts, or terminal sessions, you can enforce a specific UDP port using the --udp-port flag.

  1. Open your system terminal (Command Prompt, PowerShell, or Bash).
  2. Launch VLC with the --udp-port parameter followed by your designated port and multicast group address:
vlc --udp-port 5004 udp://@239.255.1.1

To make this change permanent for desktop shortcuts, right-click your VLC shortcut, select Properties, and append --udp-port <port_number> to the end of the Target field.
