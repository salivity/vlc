How to Enable WASAPI Exclusive Mode in VLC

Configuring VLC Media Player to use the Windows Audio Session API (WASAPI) in exclusive mode allows audiophiles to achieve bit-perfect audio playback. By bypassing the Windows system mixer (Audio Engine), VLC takes direct control of your digital-to-analog converter (DAC) or sound card. This prevents unwanted sample rate conversions, system sound interruptions, and software-level volume degradation, ensuring that your high-resolution audio files reach your ears exactly as the artist intended.

Step 1: Open Advanced Preferences in VLC

  1. Launch VLC Media Player.
  2. Click on Tools in the top menu bar and select Preferences, or press Ctrl + P on your keyboard.
  3. In the lower-left corner of the Preferences window, locate the Show settings section.
  4. Select the radio button labeled All to reveal the advanced configuration tree.

Step 2: Set the Output Module to WASAPI

  1. In the left-hand navigation pane, locate and expand the Audio category.
  2. Click directly on Output modules.
  3. On the right panel, find the Audio output module drop-down menu.
  4. Change the selection from Automatic (or DirectX) to Windows Audio Session API output.

Step 3: Enable Exclusive Mode and Select Your DAC

  1. In the left-hand navigation pane, expand the Output modules branch by clicking the arrow next to it.
  2. Select WASAPI from the sub-menu.
  3. On the right panel, locate the Output device option.
  4. Change this from Default to your specific external DAC, sound card, or audio interface.
  5. Check the box labeled Use exclusive mode (or Exclusive mode depending on the specific VLC build). This instructs Windows to give VLC sole custody over the audio hardware clock and sample rate.
  6. Click Save at the bottom right.
  7. Completely close and restart VLC Media Player for the changes to take effect.

Step 4: Configure Crucial Audiophile Settings

To guarantee bit-perfect streaming with no digital processing applied by VLC, apply these additional settings:

  • Lock Volume to 100%: Do not use the VLC volume slider below or above 100%. Lowering it reduces digital bit-depth resolution, while exceeding 100% applies digital gain that causes distortion and clipping. Control listening volume solely using your external amplifier or DAC.
  • Disable Time-Stretching: Go to Tools > Preferences > Audio (in Simple settings mode). Uncheck Enable time stretching audio. This prevents VLC from applying resampling algorithms that alter pitch or timing.
  • Disable Equalization and Audio Effects: Press Ctrl + E to open the Adjustments and Effects window. Ensure that all filters, spatializers, and equalizers under the Audio Effects tab are completely disabled.

How to Verify Exclusive Mode Is Working

To confirm the configuration is active, play an audio file in VLC and attempt to play sound from another program, such as a web browser video or a system notification. If exclusive mode is operating correctly, other applications will either produce no sound or display an audio playback error until you pause or stop playback in VLC. Furthermore, if your DAC features an integrated sample-rate display, it will automatically switch to reflect the exact native sample rate of the file currently playing.
