Does VLC Support Spatial and Surround Sound?

VLC media player fully supports surround sound playback and offers dedicated capabilities for spatial audio, though how these formats function depends heavily on your audio source and output hardware. While VLC handles standard multi-channel setups like 5.1 and 7.1 natively and includes Ambisonics-based 3D spatial audio for immersive 360-degree media, advanced object-based formats like Dolby Atmos rely on hardware passthrough rather than direct software decoding.

Surround Sound Support (5.1 and 7.1)

VLC natively decodes and plays traditional multi-channel surround sound formats, including Dolby Digital (AC-3), Dolby TrueHD, DTS, DTS-HD Master Audio, and uncompressed LPCM.

Depending on your playback environment, VLC handles surround sound in two distinct ways:

  • Direct Multi-Channel Output: If your computer is connected to a 5.1 or 7.1 speaker setup via analog audio jacks, USB, or HDMI, VLC decodes each channel internally and directs the individual audio tracks to their corresponding speakers.
  • Stereo Downmixing: If you play multi-channel content through standard stereo speakers or regular headphones, VLC automatically downmixes the surround channels to a balanced two-channel stereo feed to ensure dialogue and background effects remain audible.

Spatial Audio and 360-Degree Video

Starting with VLC 3.0, the player introduced native support for 3D spatial audio linked with 360-degree video navigation.

  • Ambisonics Audio: VLC supports higher-order Ambisonics, a spatial audio technology widely used in VR and interactive videos. When you navigate through a 360-degree video using your mouse or keyboard, VLC dynamically pans and shifts the soundstage in real time to match your field of view.
  • Binaural Headphone Rendering: When Ambisonics audio is detected and headphones are used, VLC renders the audio binaurally, simulating how human ears perceive distance, height, and horizontal direction.

Object-Based Spatial Audio: Dolby Atmos and DTS:X

VLC does not have a built-in software renderer for proprietary object-based spatial metadata, such as consumer Dolby Atmos for headphones.

  • Software Decoding Behavior: If you play an Atmos or DTS:X file directly through VLC to headphones or stereo speakers, the player decodes the underlying base track (typically Dolby TrueHD, Dolby Digital Plus, or DTS core) and downmixes it. You will hear clear surround elements mixed down to stereo, but not height-based spatial object positioning.
  • HDMI Passthrough (Bitstreaming): To experience true Dolby Atmos or DTS:X via VLC, you must enable HDMI audio passthrough. In this mode, VLC bypasses its internal decoder and sends the raw, uncompressed bitstream directly to an external Atmos-capable AV receiver or soundbar, which then renders the spatial audio channels accurately.

Built-in Audio Virtualization

For standard stereo sources, VLC includes a legacy Spatializer DSP filter located under Tools > Effects and Filters > Audio Effects. While this tool does not create true surround sound from stereo sources, it uses reverb, room size, and stereo width adjustments to simulate a wider acoustic space.

Configuring Surround Sound and Passthrough in VLC

To ensure VLC uses your multi-channel or spatial setup correctly:

  1. Open Tools > Preferences (or press Ctrl + P).
  2. Navigate to the Audio tab.
  3. Under the Output section, set the Output module to your primary system driver (such as DirectX or WASAPI on Windows).
  4. Set HDMI/SPDIF audio passthrough to Enabled if you are sending raw surround or Atmos audio to an external receiver.
  5. In the Device drop-down, select your dedicated multi-channel device or receiver.
  6. Click Save and restart VLC for the changes to apply.
